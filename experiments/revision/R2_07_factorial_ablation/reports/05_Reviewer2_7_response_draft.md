**Comment:** Separate the contributions of Gaussian smoothing, look-ahead congestion, priority adjustment, and rollback, particularly in the lifelong setting.

**Response:** Thank you for identifying the need to distinguish component contributions from the performance of the combined method. We have prepared a complete four-factor lifelong ablation using the original Lifelong CPS-A implementation and its original task-assignment and request-lifecycle logic. The design retains all 16 combinations on the STANDARD, FRANSEN and RING layouts, with 50, 150 and 120 AGVs, respectively. Each trial starts with the original 3,000-request backlogged queue and observes 500 simulation steps without a warm-up period. Thirty matched seeds per map give 1,440 planned trials. Within each map and seed, all combinations use identical initial positions and external pickup–delivery requests.

Our source audit revealed a distinction that must be made explicit in the revised manuscript: the supplied original lifelong heuristic returns the Manhattan distance before the commented look-ahead code. The implemented original baseline is therefore S1_L0_P1_R1, rather than S1_L1_P1_R1. L1 is an additional experimental treatment based on the numerical expression in that commented code, retaining the active Manhattan base. Its results can evaluate that added mechanism but cannot establish that it contributed to the original reported results. The future-window formula and its inconsistent descriptive comment are disclosed rather than silently reinterpreted.

For smoothing, S0 passes the original slot-level path-visit counts through the same field interface, while S1 applies the original Gaussian update. These counts are not binary exact-time reservation occupancy. The intervention leaves the reservation constraints, time indexing, update schedule and downstream weights unchanged. Consequently, its effect includes the changes in both propagation and magnitude introduced by the original smoothing operator; a reduction in the numerical field value alone is not evidence of better traffic performance.

The P switch controls the original dynamic priority score and the numerical priority exchange during rollback. R controls the original bounded rollback and failure-handling branch. When P is disabled but R remains enabled, the failed-agent retry order, blocker rollback and required queue operations are retained. This avoids inadvertently disabling rollback while attempting to remove priority scoring. Because priority scores also affect blocker selection, and because rollback necessarily changes retry order, their contributions are interpreted through the P×R interaction and conditional effects, not as universally additive components. The smoothing–look-ahead interaction is analyzed explicitly for the same reason.

The analysis compares each alternative with the actual original baseline and estimates the four marginal main effects, all six two-factor interactions, and predeclared P|R, R|P, S|L and L|S conditional effects. Each contrast is first computed within a matched seed, then summarized across independent seeds for each map. We report effective paired sample sizes, mean differences, 95% confidence intervals, paired tests and Holm-adjusted p values within declared comparison families. Time steps, requests and factor combinations are not treated as additional independent replications. Missing, failed, interrupted and incomplete trials remain in the reliability report and cannot be represented as complete 500-step results.

Throughput, waiting time, flow time and queue length are reported together with completion and censoring information, source-field exposure and instrumented runtime. Completed-request waiting and flow measures are accompanied by all-arrived restricted measures to reveal selection effects. The source-field units differ between the smoothed and unsmoothed variants; cross-variant numerical exposure reductions are therefore interpreted cautiously. Runtime includes the documented recording overhead. We do not infer physical safety, real-time suitability, admissibility, completeness or universal Gaussian superiority from these ablations.
